1. What is Byte Pair Encoding (BPE)?

Language models don't read words or letters directly—they operate on integer Tokens. BPE is a subword tokenization algorithm that begins with individual bytes and iteratively merges the most frequently co-occurring byte pairs in the training corpus into single tokens.

This allows models to compress common words like " the" or "function" into a single token, while gracefully breaking down unknown or rare words into smaller subword chunks or individual bytes.

2. Why does leading whitespace matter?

In modern tokenizers like cl100k_base and o200k_base, whitespace is usually attached to the front of the following word rather than the end of the previous word:

  • " hello" (with a leading space) is Token ID 24748 in o200k_base.
  • "hello" (without space) is Token ID 40617 in o200k_base!

This explains why inconsistent spaces or trailing spaces can alter token boundaries and change how LLMs attend to words.
